HSMoE: A Shapley Value-Based Mixture-of-Experts Framework for
Robust Multimodal Sentiment Analysis with Missing Modalities
Huafei Deng∗, Xuecheng Zou, Li Zhang, Yu Tang†
Abstract—Multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) is vul-
nerable to missing or corrupted modalities, whose relia-
bility may vary across samples. Existing methods often
assume equal modality contributions or treat text as per-
manently dominant, limiting their adaptability. We pro-
pose HSMoE, a Shapley value-based mixture-of-experts
framework with hierarchical dominant-modality selection
for robust MSA. HSMoE first identifies the most reliable
modality through multi-perspective confidence estima-
tion, then uses Shapley value-based routing to allocate
contributions among heterogeneous experts. An expert
reconstruction module further mitigates information loss
caused by missing inputs. Experiments on MOSI and
MOSEI, show that HSMoE achieves competitive or su-
perior performance under diverse intra- and inter-modal
missingness settings.
Index Terms— Multimodal sentiment analysis, mixture of
experts, Shapley value, missing modality.
I. INTRODUCTION
Multimodal sentiment analysis (MSA) aims to infer human
affective states from complementary textual, acoustic, and
visual signals [1, 2]. By integrating heterogeneous modali-
ties, MSA achieves more reliable sentiment recognition than
unimodal methods and has been widely applied to health-
care monitoring, human-computer interaction, and online ed-
ucation. However, real-world MSA systems frequently en-
counter missing or corrupted modalities due to sensor fail-
ures, automatic speech recognition errors, network latency,
occlusion, or privacy constraints [3, 4]. Such incomplete ob-
servations can substantially degrade prediction quality, posing
significant challenges to robust multimodal fusion.Existing
approaches to missing-modality MSA generally follow two
paradigms. Uniform fusion strategies [5, 6, 7] assume that all
modalities contribute equally and fuse them symmetrically.
Although simple, this assumption is often unreliable when
modality quality varies significantly across samples. In con-

cally treat text as the dominant modality to guide cross-modal
interaction. While effective when linguistic information is re-
liable, these methods often fail when text is corrupted or when
visual and acoustic cues are more informative.
To address the aforementioned issues, we propose the
Shapley Value-Based Mixture-of-Experts framework with
Hierarchical dominant-modality Selection (HSMoE). The
proposed framework dynamically identifies the dominant
modality and adaptively adjusts the contribution of each
modality on a per-sample basis. Specifically, HSMoE first
employs multimodal encoders to extract semantic features
from textual, visual, and auditory modalities. Subsequently,
the Hierarchical Dominant-modality Selection (HDS) mech-
anism estimates the reliability of each modality to determine
the sample-specific dominant modality. Guided by this deci-
sion, the Shapley Value-Based Mixture-of-Experts (Shapley-
MoE) strategy assigns contribution-aware weights to seven
heterogeneous experts to capture complex cross-modal inter-
actions. Furthermore, to tackle the missing modality problem,
an Expert Reconstruction (ER) module is introduced to re-
construct missing information. Finally, the reconstructed
and fused representation is fed into a multilayer perceptron
(MLP) for sentiment prediction.

V . ABLATION STUDY
Table 2 further evaluates HSMoE under different inter-modal
missing patterns on CMU-MOSI dataset. The model main-
tains stable performance across all modality combinations.
The highest Acc-2 is obtained when all three modalities are
available, while the results under partial modality availability
confirm that HSMoE can still utilize the remaining comple-
mentary information for sentiment prediction.
Table 3 reports the ablation results on MOSI. Remov-
ing any component consistently degrades performance. In
particular, removing ShapleyMoE causes the largest decline.
This result highlights the importance of ShapleyMoE. Re-
moving HDS also leads to a clear performance drop, con-
firming the benefit of sample-specific dominant-modality se-
lection. Although the removal of ER has a smaller impact,
it still decreases the overall performance, demonstrating that
feature reconstruction contributes to robustness under missing
modalities.

VI. CONCLUSION
We propose HSMoE, a Shapley value-based mixture-of-
experts framework with hierarchical dominant-modality se-
lection for missing-modality MSA. By dynamically allocat-
ing modality contributions, HSMoE achieves robust perfor-
mance on two popular MSA benchmark datasets, and we
conduct ablation studies to analyze this phenomenon and
validate the contribution of each component.
