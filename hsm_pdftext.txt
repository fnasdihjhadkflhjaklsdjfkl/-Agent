Abstract
Multimodal Sentiment Analysis (MSA) faces
significant challenges when dealing with in-
complete data, as the quality and availabil-
ity of different modalities dynamically vary
across samples.Previous methods focus on
equally treating the contribution of each modal-
ity or statically using text as the domi-
nant modality to conduct interaction, which
neglects the situation where each modal-
ity may become dominant. We propose a
novel Hierarchical dominant-modality Selec-
tion with Shapley Routing Mixture-of-Experts
framework(HS-MoE) to achieve robust MSA
under arbitrary modality-missing scenarios.
The proposed HSMoE features a Hierarchi-
cal Dominant-modality Selection and Shapley
Routing Mixture-of-Experts, which dynami-
cally adjusts the dominant modality to enhance
model robustness across diverse noisy condi-
tions. Comprehensive experiments on popu-
lar benchmark datasets (MOSI, MOSEI, and
SIMS) demonstrate that HierarchicalShapley-
MoE consistently outperforms existing base-
lines across varying missing rates and evalua-
tion metrics.

2 Related Work
2.1 Multimodal Sentiment Analysis
Multimodal Sentiment Analysis (MSA) aims to
understand and analyze human emotions by lever-
aging multiple distinct modalities. Currently, main-
stream MSA research (Tsai et al., 2019; Zadeh
et al., 2018a) primarily focuses on designing so-
phisticated fusion paradigms and interaction mech-
anisms to enhance MSA performance. However,
these methods rely heavily on complete modality
information, rendering them less feasible in prac-
tical applications. To address the issue of missing
modalities in MSA, there are currently two main
approaches:generative methods (Luo et al., 2023;
Lian et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023b) attempt to re-
construct the features and semantic information of
missing modalities by leveraging the distribution of
available ones.; and joint learning methods (Wang
et al., 2020; Zhao et al., 2021; Zeng et al., 2022;
Yuan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024)focus on con-
structing coherent multimodal joint representations
based on inter-modal correlations.
2.2 Dominant Modality
Existing research on modality fusion generally fol-
lows two distinct paradigms. Equality-based fu-
sion methods operate on the assumption that each
modality contributes equally to the final predic-
tion, employing symmetric strategies to model bidi-
rectional relationships. Early approaches utilized
tensor-based (Zadeh et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2018),
LSTM-based (Zadeh et al., 2018a), and MLP-based
(Sun et al., 2022) architectures to capture inter-
modal commonalities. These were later superseded
by transformer-based frameworks like MuLT (Tsai
et al., 2019), which align cross-modal sequences,
and subspace projection methods like MISA (Haz-
arika et al., 2020) that disentangle shared and pri-
vate features. Despite these advancements, such
symmetric methods often neglect the inherent dis-
crepancies in modality importance. In contrast,
static dominance methods posit that the textual
modality should act as the fixed anchor, guiding
the integration of acoustic and visual cues. Rep-
resentative works leverage transformers to inject
non-verbal information into text representations
(Han et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023a; Li et al.,

3 Methodology
3.1 Overall Architecture
As shown in Figure 2, which shows the overall
workflow of HSMoE framework.Specifically,the
framework first employs modality-specific en-
coders to extract high-level semantic features. Sub-
sequently, the HDS mechanism dynamically iden-
tifies the dominant modality by distilling affective
cues. Guided by this dominant modality, the SR-
MoE utilizes Shapley value-based routing to ag-
gregate outputs from seven heterogeneous experts,
effectively modeling complex multimodal depen-
dencies. Finally, the aggregated representation is
fed into a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) to regress
the final sentiment score.
3.2 Multimodal Input
To simulate scenarios with missing data, we ap-
plied a random missing strategy with varying pro-
portions (ranging from 0% to 100%) to the MOSI,
MOSEI, and SIMS datasets. Specifically, zero-
padding was applied to the erased visual and au-
dio modalities and the language modality was pop-
ulated with [UNK] tokens to indicate unknown
words in BERT (Devlin et al., 2019).
For feature extraction, we employ BERT (De-
vlin et al., 2019) to encode textual inputs, Li-
brosa (McFee et al., 2015) to extract acoustic fea-
tures, and OpenFace (Baltrusaitis et al., 2018) to ex-
tract visual features. The pre-processed inputs are
initially represented as sequences Xm ∈ RTm×dm,
where m ∈ { t, v, a} denotes the modality type
(t for text, v for visual, and a for audio). Subse-
quently, we apply a random missing strategy to
simulate incomplete modality scenarios, yielding
corrupted sequences Hm ∈ RTm×dm as the final
input representations.
3.3 hierarchical dominant-modality selection
Unlike conventional Mixture-of-Experts that
rely solely on input features for routing, our
Hierarchical Dominant-modality Selection (HDS)
explicitly decouples the representation learning
from the decision-making process. As illustrated in
Figure 2, the architecture is modularized into two
sequential stages: a Modality-Perceptive Interac-
tion module that refines features and captures cross-
modal dependencies, followed by a Confidence-
Aware Selection module that dynamically evalu-
ates the reliability of each modality to determine
the dominant expert.

3.4 Shapley Routing Mixture-of-Experts
In traditional Multimodal Mixture-of-Experts, rout-
ing is often performed via a naive softmax gate,
which fails to account for the complex interac-
tions and redundancies among experts. While
Shapley Value from cooperative game theory pro-
vides an optimal solution for credit assignment,
its exact computation suffers from factorial com-
plexity.we propose a Shapley Routing Mixture-of-
Experts(SRMoE) mechanism. This strategy lever-
ages the dominant modality identified by the HDS
module to structure the expert coalition, thereby
reducing the search space and enabling a hybrid
exact-approximate computation of Shapley Values.

4 Experiments and Analysis
4.1 Datasets
The MOSI dataset (Zadeh et al., 2016) comprises
2,199 multimodal utterances that integrate visual,
acoustic, and linguistic modalities. The dataset is
partitioned into 1,284 samples for training, 229
for validation, and 686 for testing. Each sample is
annotated with a sentiment score ranging from -3
(strongly negative) to +3 (strongly positive).
The MOSEI dataset (Zadeh et al., 2018b) con-
tains 22,856 video clips collected from YouTube.
It is split into 16,326 clips for training, 1,871 for
validation, and 4,659 for testing. Similar to MOSI,
each clip is labeled with a sentiment score on a
scale from -3 (strongly negative) to +3 (strongly
positive).
The SIMS dataset (Yu et al., 2020) is a Chi-
nese multimodal sentiment corpus consisting of
2,281 video clips extracted from various movies

Criteria

2017) toolbox with four NVIDIA GeForce RTX
3090 (24GB) GPUs. The Adam optimizer (Kingma
and Ba, 2014) is employed for network optimiza-

comparison, we re-implement the State-Of-The-

experimental paradigms. All experimental results
are averaged over multiple experiments using five
different random seeds.

4.4 Ablation Study and Analysis
To investigate the contribution of each component
in our HS-MoE framework, we conduct ablation
studies on the MOSI dataset. Specifically, we re-
move or modify three key components: the Hierar-
chical Distribution Smoothing (HDS), the Sparse
Routing Mixture-of-Experts (SRMoE), and the Ex-
pert Reconstruction (ER).
As shown in 4,removing different modules of
HS-MoE leads to a decrease in overall perfor-
mance.Specifically,The results demonstrate that re-
moving the SRMoE module leads to the most se-
vere performance degradation among all variants,
with the correlation coefficient dropping sharply
from 0.5397 to 0.4215 and the F1 score decreas-
ing by over 6. This substantial decline confirms
that SRMoE serves as the core component for cap-
turing fine-grained multimodal interactions. The
model without HDS exhibits the second-worst per-
formance,highlighting the critical role of selecting
appropriate dominant modalities across different
scenarios. Furthermore, the removal of ER also re-
sults in a noticeable performance drop, indicating
its contribution to the model’s overall robustness.

5 Conclusion
This paper proposes a Hierarchical dominant-
modality Selection with Shapley Routing Mixture-
of-Experts (HS-MoE) framework, designed to si-
multaneously address MSA tasks characterized
by both uniform and non-uniform distributions of
modality importance. By dynamically adjusting the
contribution of each modality across diverse scenar-
ios, HS-MoE effectively enhances the utilization
of the dominant modality. This mechanism renders
our model more effective and generic across three
popular MSA benchmark datasets. Finally, we con-
duct ablation studies to analyze this phenomenon
and validate the contribution of each component.
Limitations
Despite the promising performance of HS-MoE,
several limitations remain to be addressed.The em-
ployment of multiple experts combined with the
Shapley value-based routing mechanism inevitably
increases the computational cost and inference la-
tency compared to simple fusion baselines. While
effective, the calculation of Shapley values intro-
duces additional overhead that may hinder deploy-
ment in resource-constrained environments.In ad-
dition,the effectiveness of the HDS relies heavily
on the quality of modality-specific features. In
scenarios with extreme noise or severe missing
modalities where the dominant signal is ambigu-
ous, the selector might struggle to assign accurate
weights, potentially affecting the downstream fu-
sion performance.In future work, we plan to ex-
plore lightweight approximation methods for the
routing mechanism and investigate adaptive expert
pruning to improve scalability.
